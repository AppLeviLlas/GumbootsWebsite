Hahei TP58 Wastewater Specialists

TP58 wastewater design in Hahei is complicated by the area's Miocene limestone geology. The limestone bluffs and headlands that give Cathedral Cove its dramatic character create highly variable soil and rock conditions beneath Hahei's residential sections. Weathered limestone soil can have reasonable permeability in places, but fissures and solution channels in the underlying rock can also create preferential drainage pathways — meaning effluent can bypass the intended soil treatment zone and reach groundwater or the coastal marine area faster than a standard percolation test would suggest.

Rock depth is another key constraint. Where limestone is shallow, there may be insufficient soil depth above bedrock to accommodate a conventional soakage disposal field. In these situations, raised mound disposal or alternative treatment systems are often the practical solution. We assess rock depth as part of our site investigation and factor this into the disposal system design from the outset — rather than discovering it at installation time.

Properties on the beachfront and coastal flat areas of Hahei sit on coastal dune sands with high permeability and a shallow water table. WRC's coastal marine area setbacks apply along the beach frontage, limiting where disposal fields can be positioned. We assess all of these constraints together and design the most appropriate system for your specific Hahei site.
