Hahei Stormwater Design Specialists

Stormwater design in Hahei is strongly influenced by the Miocene limestone that underlies much of this part of the Coromandel Peninsula. Limestone weathers to produce variable soil profiles — from sandy residual soils with good permeability in some locations, to shallower soils over intact rock where soakpit depth is physically constrained. Where limestone fissures are present, soakpits can drain very rapidly into rock voids, which provides excellent infiltration capacity but also means that soakpit performance can be unpredictable without ground investigation to confirm the local conditions.

Hahei sits within the WRC coastal marine area, and the small coastal catchments around Cathedral Cove drain quickly to the coast. WRC's rules for stormwater discharge in the coastal environment apply, and properties close to the coast or coastal wetlands may require a resource consent for stormwater discharge as well as TCDC's building consent documentation. We advise clients early on consent pathway so there are no surprises during the building consent process.

Coastal dune sands at Hahei Beach itself are highly permeable and generally suit soakpit disposal — but seasonal groundwater rise in late winter can reduce the effective depth to groundwater, limiting the available soakpit depth. Our stormwater assessments for Hahei properties include a review of the soil and groundwater conditions to confirm whether soakpit disposal is feasible, or whether an alternative disposal method is needed.
