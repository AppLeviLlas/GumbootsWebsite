When You Need Specific Foundation Design

Standard house foundations under NZS 3604 only apply on "good ground". When a site has expansive clay, soft or variable soils, fill or a slope, council needs a foundation designed by an engineer.

  • Expansive (reactive) clay
  • Soft, wet or variable ground
  • Uncontrolled fill
  • Sloping sites, or building close to a slope
  • Heavy or unusual structures

The same applies to a granny flat or minor dwelling on difficult ground. Our planning partner Far North Resource Consents handles the granny flat consent.

Foundation Types We Design

Raft and Rib Slabs

Stiffened concrete slabs that ride out ground movement on expansive clay sites.

Piled Foundations

Timber, concrete or screw piles taken down to firmer ground.

Deepened Footings

Strip and pad footings taken below the zone of seasonal clay movement.

Pole Platforms

For steep sites where a level platform would need large cuts.

Northland Ground Conditions

Northland's weathered clays often have good bearing capacity when dry but swell and shrink as they wet and dry. Around Kerikeri, basalt ground can vary sharply in depth to rock. Good foundation design starts with knowing which you are building on.
