When Do You Need a Geotechnical Report?

A geotechnical report (sometimes called a site suitability assessment or ground investigation report) is required by councils throughout Northland for a range of building and resource consent applications. If you're unsure whether you need one, call us and we'll advise you at no charge.

Building a new home

Most building consents in Northland require a geotechnical report to confirm the ground can support your foundation design.

Subdivision & land development

Resource consent for subdivisions typically requires a geotechnical assessment of each proposed lot, including slope stability and buildability analysis. Not sure if your project needs one? Far North Resource Consents explains whether your project needs resource consent.

Sloping or challenging sites

Sites with slopes, fill, weak soils, or potential erosion risk require a geotechnical engineer's sign-off before council will approve consent.

Buying a property

A pre-purchase geotechnical report identifies ground risks before you commit — protecting you from costly surprises post-settlement.

Commercial & industrial projects

Commercial consents in Northland almost always require geotechnical input for foundation design, earthworks, and retaining structures.

Wastewater & drainage systems

Onsite wastewater (TP58) systems require soil testing as part of the design. We often combine a geotechnical report with your TP58 wastewater design to save time and cost.

What's Included in a Geotechnical Report?

Our geotechnical reports are thorough, council-ready documents prepared by qualified engineers with local Northland knowledge.

1

Site Investigation & Soil Testing

We visit your site to excavate test pits, conduct hand auger borings, and collect soil samples for laboratory analysis. Soil testing determines the properties of your ground — bearing capacity, permeability, classification — that drive the engineering recommendations.

2

Site Suitability Assessment

We assess whether your site is suitable for the proposed development, considering slope stability, flooding risk, erosion potential, and the nature of any fill or weak ground. For subdivision, we assess each proposed lot individually.

3

Foundation Recommendations

Based on the ground conditions found, we provide specific foundation design recommendations — shallow slab, pad footings, piles, or raft — along with engineering parameters your structural engineer or architect needs to complete their design. Where the site needs a fully engineered foundation, see our foundation design service.

4

Earthworks & Construction Guidance

Where earthworks, cuts, or fills are required, we include earthworks specifications and compaction requirements so your builder knows exactly how to prepare the site.

5

Council-Ready Documentation

The final report is a signed, stamped engineering document accepted by FNDC, NRC, and Whangarei District Council for building and resource consent. We understand what each council needs and write reports accordingly — minimising the chance of RFIs holding up your consent.

How Much Does a Geotechnical Report Cost in NZ?

For a standard residential site in Northland, a geotechnical report typically costs $1,500–$2,500 + GST. That covers the site investigation, soil testing, and a written report accepted by Far North District Council, Whangarei District Council, and Northland Regional Council.

More complex sites cost more. Steep or unstable ground, poor access, extensive fill, coastal hazard exposure, or projects needing deep investigation (drilling or CPT) all add scope. Lot-by-lot subdivision geotechnical assessment is priced per project — full subdivision engineering packages start from around $8,000+ for a simple two-lot split.

What affects the price?

  • Ground conditions — flat, well-characterised sites need less investigation than hillsides or soft ground
  • Level of investigation — hand augers and test pits cost less than drilling, CPT, or laboratory testing
  • Site access — remote or difficult-access sites take more time on the ground
  • Consent requirements — higher-risk zones may require additional assessment to satisfy council

How long does it take?

Being Kerikeri based, we can usually schedule a site visit within 1–3 business days for most Northland locations, and deliver the written report within 5–7 working days of the investigation. Complex assessments needing laboratory testing may take longer — we confirm timeframes up front.

Every site is different, so treat these figures as a starting point. For more detail, see our geotechnical engineering FAQ for Northland, or call us with your address and we'll give you a firm, site-specific quote — usually on the spot.
