Can Your Existing Septic Handle an Extension?

Each extra bedroom means more people and more wastewater. Where there is no council sewer, FNDC and WDC will not issue building consent until an engineer confirms the existing septic system can cope.

The request usually arrives as an RFI (request for further information) after you lodge your building consent. It typically asks for comments from a TP58 writer or chartered engineer confirming the existing onsite wastewater system can take the additional loading. The clock is then running, because council holds the application for a limited time while it waits.

What we check

The original design

We pull the original effluent report from your LIM or council file. It records the tank size, disposal field and soil type the system was built for.

What is in the ground

A site visit confirms the tank, the trenches or beds, and their condition. Old systems often differ from the paperwork.

The new daily flow

We work out the wastewater the finished house will produce from its bedroom count, using TP58 and AS/NZS 1547 design flows.

Tank and field capacity

We compare the required tank volume and disposal area with what you have, using your soil's drainage category and loading rate.

Example: Two-Bedroom Extension, Kerikeri

An owner in Kerikeri applied to turn a two-bedroom house into a four-bedroom house. FNDC sent an RFI asking whether the existing septic could take the extra load. We reviewed the 1998 effluent report, visited the site, and checked the system against the new house.

Can This Section Take a Septic System?

Buying or subdividing land without council sewer? Check that the section can take a septic system before you commit. Some sites cannot fit a disposal field that meets the setbacks, or need an advanced treatment system that costs far more than a standard septic.

A septic feasibility check looks at these points.

  • Soil type and how fast it drains
  • Room for a disposal field and a reserve area
  • Setbacks from boundaries, streams, bores and the coast
  • Slope, flooding and high groundwater
  • Whether a standard septic will do, or you need secondary treatment

If the site works, the next step is a full TP58 wastewater design for consent. Buyers can combine this check with a pre-purchase land assessment.

Septic Feasibility Questions

Do I need a new septic system for an extension?

Not always. If your tank and disposal field have spare capacity, a memo confirming that is enough for consent. If they fall short, the fix is often an extra trench or bed rather than a whole new system.

What if I can't find the original septic design?

We check the council property file first. If nothing is on record, we measure the system on site and test the soil to establish what it can take.

Does adding a bathroom or sleepout count?

Council looks at bedrooms, because bedrooms set how many people the house can hold. A sleepout with a bed usually counts as a bedroom. An extra bathroom with no extra bedrooms usually does not change the design flow.

How fast can you answer an RFI?

Send us the RFI letter, your plans and the property address. Many checks are a desktop review plus one site visit.

How much does it cost?

Every project is different, so we quote each one. Send us your details and we'll give you a free, no-obligation quote.

Can the same visit cover other RFI items?

Yes. Extension RFIs often ask for soil bearing too. We can do a bearing capacity check on the same visit.
